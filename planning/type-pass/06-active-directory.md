## Coverage gaps

- **Design→red-team-your-design is implied but not closed (11).** Module 11 produces a tiered-model design and a control matrix, but the type-10 payoff — *attack your own design and show the path now fails* — is the natural capstone move and isn't an explicit deliverable in the module. The capstone rubric ("prove it's closed") wants it; module 11 should make "re-walk PATH-001 against the tiered design, demonstrate it dies" a required step.
- **Eval Harness (13) latent in 09 and 10.** Detection (09) and posture-scoring (10) are exactly the non-deterministic systems type 13 targets: a Sigma rule set needs a labelled EVTX test corpus + a true/false-positive scorecard + a regression gate, and the PingCastle/dacledit score needs a held-baseline so "did we improve or regress?" is measured, not asserted. Both modules gesture at this (chainsaw-over-EVTX, week-over-week delta) but neither frames a held-out set + metric + CI gate. This is the track's biggest under-used type.
- **Drift / Steady-State (16) absent.** AD hardening famously decays — a new service account with an SPN, a re-added GenericWrite, a PAW that drifts. The track hardens at t=0 (10/11) but never builds the t=30 drift detector that re-scores posture and alerts on regression. Strong fit given the "if it isn't in version control it isn't real" thesis already in module 10.
- **Decision/ADR (11-type) thin.** Real AD-defence work is full of defensible tradeoff calls (Protected Users vs Authentication Policy Silos; tier-by-OU vs tier-by-group; gMSA vs LAPS vs vaulting; krbtgt rotation cadence). The track teaches the controls but never asks the learner to *choose among options under constraints and defend the pick* — no ADR deliverable anywhere.
- **No Migration/Brownfield (12).** Every real environment is a brownfield: tiering a live domain that already has DAs logging into workstations, without locking anyone out, is the actual hard job. Module 11 designs the target state on greenfield Meridian; the incremental "get there without an outage" skill is missing (and the README's own warning about "Deny log on through RDS" lockout hints at exactly this risk).

## Suggested additions

1. **Module — "AD Detection Eval Harness" (type 13, after 09).** Turn the 09 Sigma rules into eval-as-code: a labelled corpus of attack EVTX + benign-baseline EVTX, a chainsaw-driven scorecard (precision/recall, FP rate per rule), and a CI gate that fails when a rule change regresses detection or balloons false positives. Closes the highest-value gap and makes "alert vs hunt" tiering measurable rather than asserted. Likely the strongest single add to the track.

2. **Module/phase-project — "Tiering a Brownfield Domain" (type 12 + 10).** Strangler-fig migration: take the already-compromised Meridian domain and roll out the tier model *incrementally* — stage Deny-logon GPOs to a pilot OU, move Tier 0 accounts to Protected Users in waves, prove at each step that no admin is locked out and no attack path reopened. Pairs the design (11) with the real-world "without breaking prod" discipline that's currently absent, and is a natural Phase-3 project.

3. **Module — "AD Posture Drift & Steady-State" (type 16, capstone-adjacent).** A scheduled drift detector that re-runs the 10 posture audit, diffs against the committed hardened baseline, and reconciles/alerts on regressions (a freshly Kerberoastable account, a re-added dangerous ACE, krbtgt age). Operationalises the "hardening that isn't in version control will drift" thesis the track states but never builds, and gives the capstone a "stays closed" dimension beyond "is closed."
